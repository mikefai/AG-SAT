EASY — Main Purpose (1)

Passage: A study of urban bees found that gardens with native wildflowers increased pollinator visits by 40% compared to lawns.

Question: The passage primarily serves to…

A. persuade readers to replace lawns with wildflowers.
B. report a specific research finding about pollinator behavior.
C. compare urban and rural pollinator populations.
D. criticize traditional landscaping practices.

Answer: B. The passage states a research result (40% increase), not a prescription (A), not a comparison (C), not a critique (D).

MEDIUM — Transition / Function (2)

Passage excerpt: "Although solar costs have fallen, grid storage remains the bottleneck. Nevertheless, recent battery chemistries suggest a path forward."

Question: The phrase "Nevertheless, recent battery chemistries..." primarily functions to…

A. summarize the passage’s main conclusion.
B. concede a limitation then introduce a solution.
C. contrast solar with wind energy.
D. define "grid storage."

Answer: B. "Although... bottleneck" = concession; "Nevertheless..." = pivot to solution. Not a summary (A), no contrast with wind (C), not a definition (D).

HARD — Evidence / Inference (3)

Passage: A historian argues that 19th-century labor strikes were less about wages and more about time autonomy. Supporting evidence: strike demands increasingly mentioned fixed-hour clauses over pay increases after 1870.

Question: Which finding, if true, would best support the historian’s claim?

A. Wages rose faster after 1870 than before.
B. Strike demands after 1870 emphasized scheduling over salary in 78% of cases.
C. Factory owners reduced hours voluntarily.
D. Strike frequency fell after 1870.

Answer: B. Directly aligns demands (time autonomy) with the claim. A contradicts; C is irrelevant; D is about frequency not cause.
