# 🏛️ Digital SAT Reading and Writing: Full Practice Module 1

## 📋 Module Overview & Test Day Parameters
- **Test Section**: Digital SAT Reading and Writing (EBRW)
- **Item Count**: 27 Questions (Full Bluebook Module Specification)
- **Time Allocated**: 32 Minutes (~71 seconds per item)
- **Domain Distribution**:
  - *Craft and Structure* (Questions 1–7): Words in Context, Text Structure and Purpose, Cross-Text Connections
  - *Information and Ideas* (Questions 8–14): Central Ideas, Command of Evidence (Textual & Quantitative), Inferences
  - *Standard English Conventions* (Questions 15–21): Boundaries, Form, Structure, and Sense
  - *Expression of Ideas* (Questions 22–27): Transitions, Rhetorical Synthesis

## ⏱️ Section Directions
Each question in this section includes a short passage or note set followed by a single question and four options (`A`, `B`, `C`, `D`). Read each stimulus carefully and choose the best answer according to the text and Standard English conventions.

## 💡 Granular Distractor Analysis & Solution Explanations

### Questions 1–7 (Craft and Structure)
- **Q1 (B - trenchant)**: *Trenchant* means incisive, sharp, or vigorously effective in criticism. The contrast word *"While"* sets up opposition between her polite public neoclassical verses and her sharp private criticism of colonial hypocrisies. *Complimentary* (A) and *deferential* (D) create no contrast; *vacillating* (C) implies indecisiveness rather than clear critique.
- **Q2 (A - negligible)**: *Negligible* means so small or unimportant as to be disregarded. Because aerogels are 90% air pockets and exceptional thermal insulators, their conductivity is practically zero.
- **Q3 (C - substantiate)**: *Substantiate* means to provide empirical evidence to prove or validate. New archival manifests proved her once-doubted thesis. *Repudiate* (A) means to reject.
- **Q4 (A)**: Sentence 1 introduces Emma's seemingly perfect life, while Sentence 2 introduces her "real evils" (vanity, getting her way too much), qualifying the initial portrait.
- **Q5 (C)**: Text 1 treats Chicxulub as the solitary trigger; Text 2 introduces prolonged volcanic stress from the Deccan Traps beforehand. Author 2 would argue both forces operated in tandem rather than the asteroid acting alone.
- **Q6 (A)**: Summarizes the accidental mould contamination leading to Fleming's isolation of penicillin.
- **Q7 (B - precipitate)**: *Precipitate* in this context means to cause an event or action to happen suddenly or prematurely (triggering coral bleaching).

### Questions 8–14 (Information and Ideas)
- **Q8 (A)**: Captures the main finding: play isn't wasted energy, but builds prefrontal synaptic plasticity for adult adaptability.
- **Q9 (A)**: Directly proves Gallagher's hypothesis by comparing native oak (4x caterpillar biomass) to exotic ginkgo.
- **Q10 (B)**: Demonstrates that under deficit irrigation, DR-9 produces 71.4 t/ha with 17.0 kg/$m^3$ efficiency compared to Roma's 58.8 t/ha at 14.0 kg/$m^3$.
- **Q11 (A)**: The text explains that plasma dispersion delay is directly proportional to electron column density; therefore, measuring this delay reveals the quantity of ionized matter across intergalactic space.
- **Q12 (B)**: Decentralized committees had a 68% success rate vs 29% for unions because they could pivot and adjust quickly to local negotiations.
- **Q13 (A)**: Proves Sapir-Whorf by showing Language X speakers (with 2 distinct words) discriminated boundary shades in 340 ms vs 490 ms for Language Y speakers.
- **Q14 (B)**: Summarizes the need for grid batteries and HVDC lines to solve solar/wind intermittency.

### Questions 15–21 (Standard English Conventions)
- **Q15 (B)**: Two complete independent clauses separated by a semicolon (*"Johnson calculated orbital trajectories for Project Mercury; her extraordinary precision..."*).
- **Q16 (A)**: The introductory participial modifier (*"Originally synthesized in 1897..."*) describes *aspirin*. Choice A correctly follows the comma with the subject.
- **Q17 (B)**: The head subject is *"The extensive collection"* (singular), which requires the singular verb *"requires"*.
- **Q18 (A)**: A colon follows an independent clause to introduce a specific list of three plant species.
- **Q19 (C)**: Past Perfect (*"had believed"*) indicates a belief held prior to the 20th-century rediscovery.
- **Q20 (A)**: *Consequently* shows the direct causal result of the heat shield tiles protecting the airframe.
- **Q21 (B)**: Parallel series of base verbs: *submit*, *provide*, and *complete*.

### Questions 22–27 (Expression of Ideas)
- **Q22 (A - Instead)**: Contrasts the lack of photosynthesis with the replacement mechanism (chemosynthesis).
- **Q23 (C - However)**: Shifts from the machine's speed benefit to its classification errors.
- **Q24 (A - Specifically)**: Introduces the concrete example of thalidomide's R and S enantiomers.
- **Q25 (C)**: Fulfills the exact prompt goal by explaining how the permafrost $-18^\circ\text{C}$ protects seeds during power outages.
- **Q26 (B)**: Fulfills the goal by stating the exact metrics (70% tunnel boom reduction, 15% electricity savings).
- **Q27 (B)**: Directly highlights the historic 1947 milestone (first African American woman to earn a Ph.D. in chemistry in the US).
