Question 1 (Craft & Structure: Words in Context)

While her public elegies adhered strictly to neoclassical verse conventions, her private correspondence reveals a subtly ____ stance toward the hypocrisies of colonial slaveholders who championed political liberty.

Which choice completes the text with the most logical and precise word or phrase?

  • A) complimentary
  • B) trenchant
  • C) vacillating
  • D) deferential

Question 4 (Craft & Structure: Text Structure & Purpose)

Emma Woodhouse, handsome, clever, and rich, with a comfortable home and happy disposition, seemed to unite some of the best blessings of existence; and had lived nearly twenty-one years in the world with very little to distress or vex her. The real evils, indeed, of Emma's situation were the power of having rather too much her own way, and a disposition to think a little too well of herself; these were the disadvantages which threatened alloy to her many enjoyments.

Which choice best describes the function of the second sentence in the overall structure of the text?

  • A) It introduces a character flaw that qualifies the idealized depiction presented in the first sentence.
  • B) It disputes the claim that Emma enjoyed substantial wealth and high social status.
  • C) It explains the origin of Emma's familial conflicts with her father.
  • D) It predicts a specific financial disaster that will disrupt Emma's comfortable life.

Question 5 (Craft & Structure: Cross-Text Connections)

Text 1

Paleontologists have long attributed the end-Cretaceous mass extinction primarily to the catastrophic environmental shock of the Chicxulub asteroid impact. The massive release of vaporized sulfur and soot into the stratosphere triggered a prolonged "impact winter," collapsing global photosynthesizing food chains and wiping out non-avian dinosaurs within a geological instant.

Text 2

Geochronological dating of the Deccan Traps volcanic province in modern India demonstrates that massive flood basalt eruptions began hundreds of thousands of years prior to the Chicxulub impact. These sustained eruptions injected vast quantities of mercury, carbon dioxide, and sulfur dioxide into the atmosphere, causing severe global climatic instability and ecological distress long before the asteroid arrived.

Based on the texts, how would the author of Text 2 most likely respond to the characterization of the extinction event in Text 1?

  • A) By asserting that the Chicxulub asteroid never struck Earth during the Cretaceous period.
  • B) By arguing that volcanic activity from the Deccan Traps was the sole cause of the extinction, rendering the asteroid impact irrelevant.
  • C) By suggesting that pre-existing ecological stress from massive volcanism significantly contributed to the extinction rather than the asteroid acting in isolation.
  • D) By claiming that stratospheric soot from asteroid impacts warms rather than cools global climates.

  • C) Penicillium fungi are more resilient to bacterial infection than other common laboratory moulds.

Question 9 (Information & Ideas: Command of Evidence - Textual)

Ecologist Dr. Liam Gallagher investigated whether urban tree canopy composition affects the abundance of native insect pollinators. Conventional urban forestry assumes that exotic ornamental trees support equivalent pollinator diversity compared to native species. Gallagher hypothesized that native deciduous trees (such as Quercus alba) support significantly higher caterpillar biomass—a vital food source for insectivorous birds and native pollinators—than non-native ornamental cultivars (such as Ginkgo biloba).

Which finding, if true, would most directly support Dr. Gallagher's hypothesis?

  • A) Urban parks with native oak trees exhibited four times greater lepidopteran biomass and double the avian nesting density of parks dominated by non-native ginkgo trees.
  • B) Non-native ornamental trees were found to tolerate urban automotive pollutants and droughts better than native deciduous species.
  • C) Total insect biodiversity was highest in urban botanical gardens that cultivated both exotic tropical flora and native wildflowers.
  • D) Adult native bumblebees visited exotic flower species at equal frequencies when nectar concentrations were identical.

Question 10 (Information & Ideas: Command of Evidence - Quantitative)

Researchers tracked the agricultural yield and irrigation efficiency of three tomato cultivars under standard vs deficit drip irrigation.

Tomato Cultivar Irrigation Regime Water Applied ($m^3/\text{hectare}$) Fruit Yield ($\text{tons/hectare}$) Water Use Efficiency ($\text{kg fruit}/m^3\text{ water}$)
Roma Standard Full Irrigation 6,000 72.0 12.0
Roma Standard Deficit Irrigation (70%) 4,200 58.8 14.0
Drought-Resistant DR-9 Full Irrigation 6,000 78.0 13.0
Drought-Resistant DR-9 Deficit Irrigation (70%) 4,200 71.4 17.0

The agronomists concluded that while deficit irrigation reduces absolute total fruit yield across all cultivars, the drought-resistant DR-9 cultivar optimizes water productivity far more effectively than the standard cultivar under water-stressed conditions.

Which choice best uses data from the table to support the agronomists' conclusion?

  • A) Under full irrigation, Roma Standard produced 72.0 tons/hectare while using 6,000 $m^3/\text{hectare}$ of water.
  • B) Under deficit irrigation, DR-9 achieved a water use efficiency of 17.0 kg fruit/$m^3$ with a yield of 71.4 tons/ha, whereas Roma Standard produced only 58.8 tons/ha at 14.0 kg/$m^3$.
  • C) Roma Standard and DR-9 both received exactly 4,200 $m^3/\text{hectare}$ of water during the deficit irrigation regime.
  • D) DR-9 produced 78.0 tons/hectare under full irrigation and 71.4 tons/hectare under deficit irrigation.

Question 11 (Information & Ideas: Inferences)

In high-energy astrophysics, fast radio bursts (FRBs) are intense millisecond-duration pulses of radio emission originating from distant galaxies. Because the radio waves travel across billions of light-years of intergalactic plasma, lower-frequency components of the burst are delayed relative to higher frequencies—a phenomenon called plasma dispersion. The total delay is directly proportional to the column density of free electrons along the line of sight.

It can most reasonably be inferred from the text that astrophysicists can use plasma dispersion measurements of FRBs to:

  • A) Estimate the total quantity of ionized matter distributed throughout the intergalactic space between the burst source and Earth.
  • B) Prevent high-energy radio waves from dispersing when passing through terrestrial radio telescopes.
  • C) Prove that FRBs originate exclusively inside our own Milky Way galaxy.
  • D) Accelerate low-frequency radio emissions to match the velocity of high-frequency pulses.

Question 13 (Information & Ideas: Command of Evidence - Textual)

In linguistics, the "Sapir-Whorf hypothesis" proposes that the grammatical structure of a speaker's native language shapes their cognitive perception of the world. Cognitive scientist Dr. Miriam Soto tested this in color perception. In language X, distinct primary words exist for light blue (goluboy) and dark blue (siniy), whereas language Y uses a single word (blue) for both shades. Soto hypothesized that native speakers of language X would distinguish boundary shades between light and dark blue significantly faster in reaction-time discrimination tasks than native speakers of language Y.

Which finding, if true, would most directly support Dr. Soto's hypothesis?

  • A) Speakers of language X identified color changes crossing the light-blue/dark-blue boundary in 340 milliseconds on average, compared to 490 milliseconds for speakers of language Y.
  • B) Speakers of both language X and language Y exhibited identical brainwave patterns when viewing red and green light spectrums.
  • C) Language Y contains more total color terms for secondary shades like purple and orange than language X.
  • D) Children acquiring language X learn words for shapes before learning words for colors.

Question 25 (Expression of Ideas: Rhetorical Synthesis)

  • The ambient permafrost temperature maintains seed storage at $-18^\circ\text{C}$ even during local power grid failure.
  • Over 1.2 million distinct seed samples from virtually every nation on Earth are currently preserved there.

The student wants to explain how the vault ensures seed preservation during power failures. Which choice most effectively uses relevant information from the notes to accomplish this goal?

Question 26 (Expression of Ideas: Rhetorical Synthesis)

The student wants to present the specific engineering benefits achieved by the bullet train redesign. Which choice most effectively uses relevant information from the notes to accomplish this goal?

  • A) Biomimicry involves emulating natural forms, such as the wedge-shaped beak of the kingfisher bird, to resolve complex technical challenges.
  • B) By modeling the Shinkansen bullet train's nose cone after the kingfisher's beak, engineers reduced tunnel noise booms by 70% and lowered electricity consumption by 15%.
  • C) Japanese engineer Eiji Nakatsu observed that kingfishers dive into water without splashing, inspiring his work on the 500-Series Shinkansen train.
  • D) Kingfisher beaks allow the birds to dive silently into water, demonstrating principles of biomimicry used in modern Japanese transportation.

💡 Granular Distractor Analysis & Solution Explanations

Questions 1–7 (Craft and Structure)

  • Q1 (B - trenchant): Trenchant means incisive, sharp, or vigorously effective in criticism. The contrast word "While" sets up opposition between her polite public neoclassical verses and her sharp private criticism of colonial hypocrisies. Complimentary (A) and deferential (D) create no contrast; vacillating (C) implies indecisiveness rather than clear critique.
  • Q2 (A - negligible): Negligible means so small or unimportant as to be disregarded. Because aerogels are 90% air pockets and exceptional thermal insulators, their conductivity is practically zero.
  • Q3 (C - substantiate): Substantiate means to provide empirical evidence to prove or validate. New archival manifests proved her once-doubted thesis. Repudiate (A) means to reject.
  • Q4 (A): Sentence 1 introduces Emma's seemingly perfect life, while Sentence 2 introduces her "real evils" (vanity, getting her way too much), qualifying the initial portrait.
  • Q5 (C): Text 1 treats Chicxulub as the solitary trigger; Text 2 introduces prolonged volcanic stress from the Deccan Traps beforehand. Author 2 would argue both forces operated in tandem rather than the asteroid acting alone.
  • Q6 (A): Summarizes the accidental mould contamination leading to Fleming's isolation of penicillin.
  • Q7 (B - precipitate): Precipitate in this context means to cause an event or action to happen suddenly or prematurely (triggering coral bleaching).

Questions 8–14 (Information and Ideas)

  • Q8 (A): Captures the main finding: play isn't wasted energy, but builds prefrontal synaptic plasticity for adult adaptability.
  • Q9 (A): Directly proves Gallagher's hypothesis by comparing native oak (4x caterpillar biomass) to exotic ginkgo.
  • Q10 (B): Demonstrates that under deficit irrigation, DR-9 produces 71.4 t/ha with 17.0 kg/$m^3$ efficiency compared to Roma's 58.8 t/ha at 14.0 kg/$m^3$.
  • Q11 (A): The text explains that plasma dispersion delay is directly proportional to electron column density; therefore, measuring this delay reveals the quantity of ionized matter across intergalactic space.
  • Q12 (B): Decentralized committees had a 68% success rate vs 29% for unions because they could pivot and adjust quickly to local negotiations.
  • Q13 (A): Proves Sapir-Whorf by showing Language X speakers (with 2 distinct words) discriminated boundary shades in 340 ms vs 490 ms for Language Y speakers.
  • Q14 (B): Summarizes the need for grid batteries and HVDC lines to solve solar/wind intermittency.

Questions 15–21 (Standard English Conventions)

  • Q15 (B): Two complete independent clauses separated by a semicolon ("Johnson calculated orbital trajectories for Project Mercury; her extraordinary precision...").
  • Q16 (A): The introductory participial modifier ("Originally synthesized in 1897...") describes aspirin. Choice A correctly follows the comma with the subject.
  • Q17 (B): The head subject is "The extensive collection" (singular), which requires the singular verb "requires".
  • Q18 (A): A colon follows an independent clause to introduce a specific list of three plant species.
  • Q19 (C): Past Perfect ("had believed") indicates a belief held prior to the 20th-century rediscovery.
  • Q20 (A): Consequently shows the direct causal result of the heat shield tiles protecting the airframe.
  • Q21 (B): Parallel series of base verbs: submit, provide, and complete.

Questions 22–27 (Expression of Ideas)

  • Q22 (A - Instead): Contrasts the lack of photosynthesis with the replacement mechanism (chemosynthesis).
  • Q23 (C - However): Shifts from the machine's speed benefit to its classification errors.
  • Q24 (A - Specifically): Introduces the concrete example of thalidomide's R and S enantiomers.
  • Q25 (C): Fulfills the exact prompt goal by explaining how the permafrost $-18^\circ\text{C}$ protects seeds during power outages.
  • Q26 (B): Fulfills the goal by stating the exact metrics (70% tunnel boom reduction, 15% electricity savings).
  • Q27 (B): Directly highlights the historic 1947 milestone (first African American woman to earn a Ph.D. in chemistry in the US).
