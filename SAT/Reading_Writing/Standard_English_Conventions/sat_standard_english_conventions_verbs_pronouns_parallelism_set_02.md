## 💡 Granular Distractor Analysis & Explanations

### Question 1
- **Correct Answer: B (*stand*)**
  - **Inverted Syntax Rule**: The prepositional phrase *"Surrounding the perimeter of the ancient Persian ceremonial complex of Persepolis"* is the locative predicate. The true subject comes AFTER the verb: *"several imposing limestone colossi"* (plural). A plural subject requires the plural present verb `stand`.
  - **Distractors (A, C, D)**: Singular verbs (*stands, was standing, has stood*) that erroneously agree with *"complex"* or *"Persepolis"*.

### Question 2
- **Correct Answer: B (*their*)**
  - **Antecedent Rule**: The pronoun refers back to *"its blastema cells"* (plural noun). Plural antecedent requires the plural possessive pronoun `their`.
  - **Distractor A (*its*)**: Singular possessive; fails to match plural *"cells"*.
  - **Distractor D (*it's*)**: Contraction for *it is*, which is grammatically impossible before a noun phrase.

### Question 3
- **Correct Answer: A (*subjected*)**
  - **Inverted Third Conditional Rule**: The formal inversion for an unfulfilled past condition is: `Had + Subject + Past Participle (V3)`. With subject *"the aerospace engineers"*, the past participle is `subjected`.
  - **Distractor D (*have subjected*)**: Double auxiliary (*Had ... have subjected*) is ungrammatical.

### Question 4
- **Correct Answer: B (*to penalize*)**
  - **Correlative Parallelism Rule**: Elements joined by `not only [X] but also [Y]` must share identical grammatical form. Since element X is an infinitive phrase (*to incentivize municipal adoption...*), element Y must also be an infinitive phrase (*to penalize industrial emissions...*).

### Question 5
- **Correct Answer: C (*is*)**
  - **Indefinite Pronoun Rule**: In formal Standard English, `Neither` when used as the subject pronoun is singular (*"Neither of the [plural noun] is..."*). The intervening phrase *"of the two autonomous deep-sea research submersibles"* is a modifier. Singular present verb `is` is required.
  - **Distractors (A, B, D)**: Plural verbs (*are, were, have been*).
