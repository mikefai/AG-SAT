## ❓ Question Items

### Question 1 (Art History: Restoration vs. Preservation)
**Text 1**  
For centuries, art conservators prioritized making damaged Renaissance paintings look visually pristine, often utilizing invasive chemical varnishes and repainting degraded pigment layers. Modern preservationists argue that this aggressive interventionist ethos distorts the artist’s original work. True conservation, they contend, must be strictly non-invasive: its goal should be stabilizing physical decay rather than cosmetically recreating vanished brushstrokes.

**Text 2**  
While non-invasive stabilization prevents accidental damage, an overly rigid refusal to reconstruct lost imagery diminishes the public’s aesthetic engagement. Advanced non-destructive spectroscopic imaging and reversible synthetic polymers now allow conservators to digitally map missing pigment layers and apply micro-restorations that can be cleanly dissolved without altering original canvas fibers. To insist on leaving major visual fractures untouched is to prioritize an abstract preservation dogma over the experiential power of art.

Based on the texts, how would the author of Text 2 most likely respond to the argument in Text 1 regarding non-invasive conservation?

- **A)** By agreeing that chemical varnishes used in earlier centuries were completely harmless to delicate Renaissance canvases.
- **B)** By contending that modern reversible restoration methods allow for aesthetic reconstruction without risking permanent damage to the original work.
- **C)** By claiming that museum visitors prefer viewing fractured paintings over cosmetically restored masterpieces.
- **D)** By suggesting that non-destructive spectroscopic imaging makes physical conservation work entirely unnecessary.

### Question 2 (Behavioral Economics: Rational Choice vs. Nudge Theory)
**Text 1**  
Neoclassical economic models presume that individual consumers act as strictly rational agents who evaluate all available market information to maximize personal utility. According to this framework, government regulations designed to steer consumer behavior are unnecessary and distortive; providing transparent price and product data is sufficient for markets to achieve optimal efficiency.

**Text 2**  
Behavioral economists have empirically demonstrated that cognitive biases, decision fatigue, and heuristic shortcuts consistently lead individuals to make suboptimal financial and dietary choices, even when complete information is available. Consequently, subtle institutional "nudges"—such as automatically enrolling employees into retirement savings plans unless they explicitly opt out—drastically improve long-term welfare while preserving individual freedom of choice.

Based on the texts, what would the author of Text 2 most likely say about the neoclassical assumption described in Text 1?

- **A)** It correctly explains why automatic enrollment programs fail to increase retirement savings.
- **B)** It overlooks the profound impact of human cognitive limitations and psychological biases on everyday decision-making.
- **C)** It underestimates the mathematical complexity of consumer utility calculation algorithms.
- **D)** It accurately predicts consumer spending habits during periods of rapid macroeconomic inflation.

### Question 3 (Neuroscience: Brain Plasticity & Critical Periods)
**Text 1**  
Classical neurodevelopmental theory asserted that the mammalian brain undergoes fixed "critical periods" in early development during which sensory and linguistic neural circuitry is permanently wired. Once these juvenile windows close, the structural architecture of the adult cerebral cortex was believed to be essentially immutable, rendering comprehensive recovery from severe neurological injury impossible.

**Text 2**  
Recent investigations utilizing optogenetics and two-photon calcium imaging reveal that the adult brain retains a remarkable degree of neuroplastic reorganization. When targeted non-invasive sensory therapies or specific neurochemical modulators are administered, adult neural pathways can form new dendritic spines and re-route signal transmission around ischemic lesions, demonstrating that neural malleability extends well into adulthood.

Based on the texts, the findings described in Text 2 challenge the theory in Text 1 by showing that:

- **A)** critical developmental periods in juveniles last much longer than classical theorists originally estimated.
- **B)** linguistic acquisition occurs more rapidly in adults than in young children.
- **C)** the adult mammalian brain possesses dynamic structural adaptability rather than remaining completely fixed.
- **D)** optogenetic therapies are ineffective when applied to juvenile cerebral cortex development.

### Question 4 (Ecology: Rewilding & Apex Predators)
**Text 1**  
Proponents of trophic rewilding advocate reintroducing apex predators, such as wolves, into degraded wilderness reserves. They point to the "ecology of fear" hypothesis: apex predators not only regulate overabundant herbivore populations through predation but also alter herbivore grazing movements, allowing overbrowsed riparian plant communities to regenerate and restoring hydrological balance.

**Text 2**  
Long-term ecological monitoring in sub-arctic ecosystems cautions against oversimplifying trophic cascades. In many landscapes, soil nutrient depletion, climate-induced drought, and changing fire regimes exert far greater control over vegetation recovery than predator presence alone. In the absence of favorable abiotic soil conditions, reintroducing apex predators produces negligible vegetative regeneration.

Based on the texts, how does Text 2 qualify the claims made in Text 1?

- **A)** By demonstrating that apex predators accelerate vegetation loss in riparian zones.
- **B)** By arguing that abiotic environmental factors like soil nutrients and drought can constrain the positive ecological impacts of predator reintroduction.
- **C)** By proving that wolves completely fail to alter herbivore grazing movements in sub-arctic regions.
- **D)** By showing that herbivore populations never recover once apex predators are reintroduced.

## 💡 Granular Distractor Analysis & Solution Explanations

### Question 1
- **Correct Answer: B**
  - **Text Evidence**: Text 1 claims interventionist restoration distorts art and insists on strict non-intervention. Text 2 counters that modern technology uses "reversible synthetic polymers" and "spectroscopic imaging" that can be "cleanly dissolved without altering original canvas fibers," enabling aesthetic reconstruction without permanent risk.
- **Distractor Analysis**:
  - **A**: Text 2 does not defend historical chemical varnishes.
  - **C**: Text 2 argues public engagement is diminished by fractured images, not that visitors prefer fractures.
  - **D**: Text 2 sees imaging as a tool *for* physical restoration, not a replacement for conservation.

### Question 2
- **Correct Answer: B**
  - **Text Evidence**: Text 1 assumes consumers are rational agents needing only transparent data. Text 2 demonstrates that "cognitive biases, decision fatigue, and heuristic shortcuts" cause individuals to make suboptimal choices despite having full information.
- **Distractor Analysis**:
  - **A**: Text 2 states automatic enrollment *drastically improves* savings.
  - **C**: Focus is on human psychology, not mathematical algorithm complexity.
  - **D**: Macroeconomic inflation is never mentioned in either text.

### Question 3
- **Correct Answer: C**
  - **Text Evidence**: Text 1 claimed adult brains were "essentially immutable." Text 2 demonstrates that adult brains form new dendritic spines and re-route signals around lesions, proving dynamic structural plasticity.
- **Distractor Analysis**:
  - **A**: Text 2 focuses on adult plasticity, not juvenile window duration.
  - **B**: Language acquisition speed is not compared between age groups.
  - **D**: Optogenetics is shown to be effective, not ineffective.

### Question 4
- **Correct Answer: B**
  - **Text Evidence**: Text 1 claims apex predators restore ecosystems through trophic cascades. Text 2 qualifies this by showing that without proper abiotic conditions (soil nutrients, water), predator presence produces negligible vegetation recovery.
- **Distractor Analysis**:
  - **A**: Text 2 does not claim predators accelerate loss.
  - **C**: Extreme claim; Text 2 focuses on abiotic limitations, not denying movement changes entirely.
  - **D**: Text 2 does not discuss herbivore extinction.
