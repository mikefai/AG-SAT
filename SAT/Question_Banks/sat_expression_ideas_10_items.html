Expression of Ideas — 35 Practice Items

Q1 — Transition (Easy)

Sentences: (1) Costs fell. (2) Storage remains bottleneck.

Question: Best connector:

  • A. Because
  • B. However
  • C. For example
  • D. In addition
Answer: B. contrast/contrast relation).

Q2 — Rhetorical Synthesis (Medium)

Passage: The new bus route is efficient. The new bus route is also affordable for students.

Question: Which choice most effectively combines the sentences?

  • A. The new bus route is efficient but students cannot afford it.
  • B. The new bus route is both efficient and affordable for students.
  • C. The new bus route is efficient, and affordability is a thing for students.
  • D. Efficient and affordable are words describing the new bus route for students.
Answer: B. Combines both ideas smoothly without repetition. C is wordy and awkward; D is vague; A contradicts the original meaning.

Q3 — Organization (Medium)

Passage: An essay draft is ordered: (1) proposed solution, (2) background context, (3) statement of the problem, (4) conclusion.

Question: Which ordering most improves the logical flow?

  • A. Conclusion → proposed solution → statement of the problem → background context.
  • B. Statement of the problem → conclusion → proposed solution → background context.
  • C. Background context → statement of the problem → proposed solution → conclusion.
  • D. Proposed solution → background context → statement of the problem → conclusion.
Answer: C. Context first, then problem, solution, and conclusion is the coherent arc. D opens with a solution before the problem; A leads with the conclusion; B buries the solution after the conclusion.

Q4 — Transition (Medium)

"Results were mixed. _____, further testing is needed."

  • A. Therefore
  • B. Nevertheless
  • C. Because
  • D. For instance
Answer: B. concession/contrast); A implies certainty wrong; C requires cause.

Q5 — Synthesis (Hard)

Passage: Sentence 1: The policy reduced emissions by 12%. Sentence 2: Compliance costs rose sharply for small firms.

Question: Which choice most effectively combines the sentences while preserving both ideas?

  • A. Compliance costs rose sharply, so emissions were reduced by 12%.
  • B. The policy failed because small firms paid costs.
  • C. Although the policy reduced emissions by 12%, compliance costs rose sharply for small firms.
  • D. The policy reduced emissions by 12% and small firms exist.
Answer: C. Preserves both facts with the correct concessive relationship. D drops the cost increase; A reverses causation; B adds an unsupported judgment of failure.

Q6 — Sentence Boundaries (Medium)

Passage: The manager, who started in 2019, approved the plan.

Question: Which choice best completes the sentence?

  • A. The manager; who started in 2019 approved the plan.
  • B. The manager who started, in 2019 approved the plan.
  • C. The manager, who started in 2019, approved the plan. (No change)
  • D. The manager who started in 2019 approved, the plan.
Answer: C. The commas correctly set off the nonrestrictive clause. D creates a comma splice; A misuses a semicolon before a dependent clause; B splits the clause incorrectly.

Q7 — Transition (Hard)

"Data supports the model. _____, predictions remain uncertain due to sparse inputs."

  • A. Consequently
  • B. However
  • C. Thus
  • D. For example
Answer: B. contrast between support and uncertainty).

Q8 — Organization (Medium)

Passage: A student essay ends with its main claim: "School gardens improve both nutrition and science scores." The body paragraphs present evidence for this claim but never state it upfront.

Question: Which revision most improves coherence?

  • A. Move the conclusion to the middle of the essay.
  • B. Add three unrelated anecdotes to the introduction.
  • C. Move the main claim to the introduction so readers know the point from the start.
  • D. Delete all evidence paragraphs and keep only the conclusion.
Answer: C. Stating the claim early frames the evidence that follows. D removes support; A buries the ending mid-essay; B adds irrelevant material.

Q9 — Rhetorical Synthesis (Easy)

Passage: Volunteers helped clean the parks. As a result, the parks look much better.

Question: Which choice most effectively combines the sentences?

  • A. The parks look much better, but no volunteers helped.
  • B. Cleaning and looking are activities people do.
  • C. Volunteers helped clean the parks, so the parks look much better.
  • D. Volunteers and parks are things that exist in the city.
Answer: C. Preserves the causal link simply. D and B are vague; A contradicts the original.

Q10 — Transition (Hard)

Passage: The first paragraph argues that education raises earnings. The next paragraph presents cases where earnings stayed low despite more schooling.

Question: Which transition best begins the second paragraph?

  • A. For example, schooling always raises earnings.
  • B. Nevertheless, several cases show earnings staying low despite more schooling.
  • C. Therefore, education has no exceptions.
  • D. Because earnings stayed low, education raises earnings.
Answer: B. "Nevertheless" signals the counterexample relationship. A agrees instead of contrasting; C overstates with "no exceptions"; D creates circular causation.

Q11 — Transition (Easy)

Passage: The first experiment failed. _____, the team revised the procedure and tried again.

Question: Which choice best completes the text?

  • A. For example
  • B. Likewise
  • C. Therefore
  • D. However
Answer: C. Failure → revision is cause and effect. D needs contrast; A needs an illustration; B needs similarity.

Q12 — Synthesis (Medium)

Passage: Sentence 1: The bridge shortened commutes by 20 minutes. Sentence 2: Tolls angered daily drivers.

Question: Which choice most effectively combines the sentences?

  • A. Tolls shortened commutes by 20 minutes.
  • B. Drivers angered the bridge with tolls.
  • C. Although the bridge shortened commutes by 20 minutes, tolls angered daily drivers.
  • D. The bridge shortened commutes and drivers were happy about tolls.
Answer: C. Keeps both facts with the concessive link. D falsely claims happiness; A swaps subject and object; B is nonsensical.

Q13 — Organization (Medium)

Passage: A paragraph describes the festival's closing ceremony before mentioning its opening parade.

Question: Which revision most improves coherence?

  • A. Move the ceremony to the middle.
  • B. Add an unrelated concert review.
  • C. Reorder chronologically: parade first, ceremony last.
  • D. Delete the opening parade entirely.
Answer: C. Chronology restores natural order. D deletes relevant content; A keeps disorder; B adds irrelevance.

Q14 — Transition (Medium)

Passage: "The soil was rich and rainfall was steady. _____, the harvest broke records."

Question: Which choice best completes the text?

  • A. Nevertheless
  • B. Consequently
  • C. Despite this
  • D. For instance
Answer: B. Good conditions → record harvest is cause and effect. A and C need contrast; D needs an example, not a result.

Q15 — Synthesis (Hard)

Passage: Sentence 1: The vaccine is highly effective. Sentence 2: Cold-chain storage limits its use in remote clinics.

Question: Which choice most effectively combines the sentences?

  • A. The vaccine is ineffective and needs no storage.
  • B. Remote clinics invented the vaccine.
  • C. Cold chains cause vaccines to work everywhere.
  • D. While highly effective, the vaccine's cold-chain needs limit remote use.
Answer: D. Preserves efficacy plus the limitation with correct logic. A inverts efficacy; B invents authorship; C overstates with "everywhere."

Q16 — Boundaries (Medium)

Passage: The committee approved the budget, _____ the new playground will be built by spring.

Question: Which choice completes the text correctly?

  • A. ; however,
  • B. , however
  • C. . However,
  • D. and
Answer: D. Simple coordination joins two related clauses. A and C need contrast that isn't there; B is a comma splice.

Q17 — Transition (Hard)

Passage: "Critics predicted the festival would flop. _____, attendance doubled and vendors sold out."

Question: Which choice best completes the text?

  • A. Therefore
  • B. Moreover
  • C. In contrast
  • D. Because
Answer: C. Prediction vs outcome is direct contrast. A and D imply the flop caused success; B merely adds without the reversal.

Q18 — Focus (Medium)

Passage: An essay about migratory birds spends two paragraphs on the author's favorite binoculars.

Question: Which revision best keeps the focus?

  • A. Add three more paragraphs about binoculars.
  • B. Move the binoculars to the conclusion.
  • C. Replace birds with binoculars as the topic.
  • D. Cut the binocular paragraphs and expand the migration evidence.
Answer: D. Removes the digression, restores the topic. A–C all entrench the off-topic material.

Q19 — Synthesis (Easy)

Passage: The train was delayed. Passengers waited on the platform.

Question: Which choice most effectively combines the sentences?

  • A. The train waited because passengers were delayed.
  • B. Platforms delay trains and passengers.
  • C. Waiting is a train that passengers platform.
  • D. Because the train was delayed, passengers waited on the platform.
Answer: D. Clean causal combination. A swaps cause and effect; B and C garble meaning.

Q20 — Transition (Medium)

Passage: "The data were incomplete. _____, the committee postponed its recommendation."

Question: Which choice best completes the text?

  • A. Nevertheless
  • B. Therefore
  • C. Likewise
  • D. For example
Answer: B. Incomplete data → postponement is cause and effect. A needs contrast; C needs similarity; D needs illustration.

Q21 — Precision (Hard)

Passage: The report says the chemical "killed some cells in a dish," but the headline claims it "cures disease."

Question: Which revision makes the headline accurate?

  • A. "Chemical cures all disease."
  • B. "Dishes cure disease."
  • C. "Scientists cure headlines."
  • D. "Chemical kills some cells in lab dish; human effects unknown."
Answer: D. Matches the evidence scope exactly. A repeats the hype; B and C are nonsensical.

Q22 — Organization (Easy)

Passage: A recipe lists "serve hot" before the ingredients.

Question: Which order is most logical?

  • A. Serve → ingredients → steps.
  • B. Steps → serve → ingredients.
  • C. Serve → serve → serve.
  • D. Ingredients → steps → serve.
Answer: D. Standard procedural order. A–C all serve before cooking.

Q23 — Transition (Medium)

Passage: "Wolves returned to the valley. _____, deer overgrazing declined."

Question: Which choice best completes the text?

  • A. However
  • B. For instance
  • C. In other words
  • D. As a result
Answer: D. Predator return → less overgrazing is cause and effect. A needs contrast; B needs an example; C needs restatement.

Q24 — Synthesis (Medium)

Passage: Sentence 1: Solar panels are cheap to run. Sentence 2: Installation costs deter homeowners.

Question: Which choice most effectively combines the sentences?

  • A. Solar panels are free to install and run.
  • B. Homeowners deter installation by running.
  • C. Running is cheap, so installation is free.
  • D. Though cheap to run, solar panels' installation costs deter homeowners.
Answer: D. Concessive link preserves both facts. A and C erase the cost; B garbles agency.

Q25 — Redundancy (Medium)

Passage: "The unexpected surprise shocked everyone completely."

Question: Which choice is most concise without losing meaning?

  • A. The surprise shocked everyone.
  • B. The unexpected surprise shocked everyone completely and totally.
  • C. Everyone was shocked by the surprising unexpectedness.
  • D. Shocking surprises shock shocking people.
Answer: A. "Unexpected surprise" and "completely shocked" are redundant. B adds more redundancy; C and D stay wordy.

Q26 — Transition (Hard)

Passage: "The theory predicts steady warming. _____, the last decade shows a plateau the theory did not anticipate."

Question: Which choice best completes the text?

  • A. Consistent with this
  • B. Unexpectedly
  • C. Therefore
  • D. For example
Answer: B. Plateau vs prediction is a surprise needing concession. A claims agreement; C claims causation; D offers no illustration.

Q27 — Conclusion (Medium)

Passage: An essay details falling river levels, failed crops, and empty reservoirs, then ends by describing a new dam's ribbon-cutting.

Question: Which ending best fits the essay's logic?

  • A. A warning that supply projects alone cannot fix demand and drought.
  • B. A recipe for reservoir cake.
  • C. A list of ribbon colors.
  • D. Praise for scissors.
Answer: A. Follows the buildup of scarcity evidence. B–D abandon the topic entirely.

Q28 — Transition (Easy)

Passage: "She saved for months. _____, she bought the laptop in cash."

Question: Which choice best completes the text?

  • A. Nevertheless
  • B. Finally
  • C. However
  • D. Despite this
Answer: B. Saving → purchase is a sequence climax. A, C, D all need contrast that isn't there.

Q29 — Synthesis (Hard)

Passage: Sentence 1: Remote sensors monitor the forest. Sentence 2: Rangers still patrol because sensors miss poachers under canopy.

Question: Which choice most effectively combines the sentences?

  • A. Although remote sensors monitor the forest, rangers still patrol since sensors miss poachers under canopy.
  • B. Sensors patrol and rangers monitor.
  • C. Canopy poachers monitor sensors.
  • D. Rangers replaced all sensors permanently.
Answer: A. Keeps monitoring fact, patrol fact, and reason. B swaps roles; C is nonsense; D contradicts "still patrol."

Q30 — Emphasis (Medium)

Passage: A paragraph buries its main finding — "the drug failed" — in the middle of dosage details.

Question: Which revision best emphasizes the finding?

  • A. Open with "The drug failed," then give dosage details.
  • B. Hide the finding in a footnote.
  • C. Delete the finding and keep dosages.
  • D. Move dosages to the title.
Answer: A. Fronting the key result gives it emphasis. B–D all suppress it further.

Q31 — Transition (Medium)

Passage: "The streets flooded every spring. _____, the council approved storm drains."

Question: Which choice best completes the text?

  • A. In response
  • B. Nevertheless
  • C. For example
  • D. Similarly
Answer: A. Drains answer flooding — response relationship. B needs contrast; C needs illustration; D needs similarity.

Q32 — Consistency (Easy)

Passage: A manual switches randomly between "you" and "one" when giving instructions.

Question: Which choice best fixes the inconsistency?

  • A. Use "you" throughout the instructions.
  • B. Alternate randomly for variety.
  • C. Remove all pronouns and all verbs.
  • D. Switch to past tense halfway.
Answer: A. Consistent second person. B preserves the flaw; C guts the manual; D adds a new inconsistency.

Q33 — Transition (Hard)

Passage: "Test scores rose. _____, critics note the gains came from easier exams, not better learning."

Question: Which choice best completes the text?

  • A. However
  • B. Therefore
  • C. Moreover, scores rose more
  • D. Because learning improved
Answer: A. The critics' point undercuts the rise — concession. B and D credit learning; C merely piles on.

Q34 — Introduction (Medium)

Passage: An article about vaccine logistics opens with three paragraphs on the author's childhood pets.

Question: Which opening best fits the topic?

  • A. A cold-chain failure that spoiled 1,000 doses last winter.
  • B. More paragraphs about pets.
  • C. A poem about cats.
  • D. No introduction at all.
Answer: A. Leading with a logistics failure frames the topic instantly. B and C extend the digression; D removes framing entirely.

Q35 — Full Revision (Hard)

Passage: Draft order: conclusion first, evidence second, background last, with the claim missing.

Question: Which revision best repairs the draft?

  • A. Add the claim up front, then background, evidence, conclusion.
  • B. Keep the order but delete the evidence.
  • C. Move background to the end and drop the conclusion.
  • D. Add unrelated jokes throughout.
Answer: A. Restores claim-first arc with logical flow. B–D all remove structure instead of repairing it.
