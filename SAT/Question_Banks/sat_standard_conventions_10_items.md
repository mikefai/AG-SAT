# Standard English Conventions — 35 Practice Items

**Flow:** Teach → Show → Test

---

## Q1 — Dangling Modifier (Easy)

**Passage:** Walking to school, the rain started pouring.

**Question:** Which choice corrects the dangling modifier?

- A. Walking to school was when the rain started pouring.

- B. As I walked to school, the rain started pouring.

- C. Walking to school, the rain started pouring.

- D. The rain started pouring walking to school.

**Answer:** B. The modifier now attaches to "I," the one walking. C is unchanged; D still dangles; A leaves the walker unclear.

---

## Q2 — Subject-Verb Agreement (Medium)

**Passage:** The team of researchers are publishing their findings tomorrow.

**Question:** Which choice conforms to Standard English?

- A. The team of researchers be publishing their findings tomorrow.

- B. The team of researchers is publishing its findings tomorrow.

- C. The team of researchers are publishing their findings tomorrow.

- D. The team of researchers were publishing their findings tomorrow.

**Answer:** B. "Team" is a singular collective noun requiring "is." C keeps the plural verb; D shifts to past tense; A is ungrammatical.

---

## Q3 — Pronoun Agreement (Medium)

**Passage:** Each student must submit their own report by Friday.

**Question:** Which choice best maintains pronoun agreement in formal writing?

- A. Each student must submit them own report by Friday.

- B. Each student must submit his or her own report by Friday.

- C. Each student must submit its own report by Friday.

- D. Each student must submit our own report by Friday.

**Answer:** B. "His or her" agrees with singular "each student." C uses "its" for people; D shifts person to "our"; A is ungrammatical.

---

## Q4 — Parallelism (Medium)

**Passage:** She likes running, to swim, and cycling.

**Question:** Which choice maintains parallel structure?

- A. She likes running, to swim, and to cycle.

- B. She likes running, swimming, and cycling.

- C. She likes running, to swim, and cycling.

- D. She likes to run, swimming, and cycling.

**Answer:** B. All three items are parallel gerunds. C–A mix infinitives and gerunds.

---

## Q5 — Comma Splice (Medium)

**Passage:** The meeting starts at 10 a.m. however the agenda just changed.

**Question:** Which choice corrects the run-on?

- A. The meeting starts at 10 a.m. however; the agenda just changed.

- B. The meeting starts at 10 a.m.; however, the agenda just changed.

- C. The meeting starts at 10 a.m. however, the agenda just changed.

- D. The meeting starts at 10 a.m., however the agenda just changed.

**Answer:** B. Two independent clauses joined with semicolon + conjunctive adverb. C has no boundary punctuation; D is a comma splice; A misplaces the semicolon.

---

## Q6 — Nonrestrictive Clause (Hard)

**Passage:** The old factory built in 1921 was renovated last year.

**Question:** The clause "built in 1921" is extra information about a factory already identified. Which choice punctuates it correctly?

- A. The old factory built, in 1921 was renovated last year.

- B. The old factory, built in 1921, was renovated last year.

- C. The old factory built in 1921 was renovated last year.

- D. The old factory; built in 1921, was renovated last year.

**Answer:** B. Nonrestrictive clauses take paired commas. C treats the clause as restrictive, changing the meaning; D misuses a semicolon; A splits the clause incorrectly.

## Q7 — Ambiguous Pronoun (Medium)

**Passage:** The committee told the volunteers that they would review the proposal on Monday.

**Question:** It is unclear who "they" refers to. Which choice removes the ambiguity?

- A. The committee told the volunteers that them would review the proposal on Monday.

- B. The committee told the volunteers that it would review the proposal on Monday.

- C. The committee told the volunteers that they would review the proposal on Monday.

- D. The committee told the volunteers that those would review the proposal on Monday.

**Answer:** B. "It" clearly refers to the singular committee. C keeps the ambiguity; D and A are ungrammatical.

## Q8 — Agreement With Interrupter (Hard)

**Passage:** The list of requirements, including safety checks, are long.

**Question:** Which choice is correct? (The subject is "list.")

- A. The list of requirements, including safety checks, be long.

- B. The list of requirements, including safety checks, is long.

- C. The list of requirements, including safety checks, are long.

- D. The list of requirements, including safety checks, were long.

**Answer:** B. The singular subject "list" needs "is"; the interrupting phrase does not change agreement. C agrees with "checks"; D shifts tense; A is ungrammatical.

## Q9 — Introductory Phrase (Medium)

**Passage:** After the concert we went home and ate pizza.

**Question:** Which choice correctly punctuates the introductory phrase?

- A. After the concert; we went home and ate pizza.

- B. After: the concert we went home and ate pizza.

- C. After the concert, we went home and ate pizza.

- D. After the concert we went home and ate pizza.

**Answer:** C. Introductory phrases take a following comma. D omits it; A misuses a semicolon after a fragment; B misuses a colon.

## Q10 — Dangling Modifier (Medium)

**Passage:** Having studied for weeks, the exam felt easy.

**Question:** Which choice corrects the error?

- A. The exam, having studied for weeks, felt easy.

- B. Having studied for weeks was why the exam felt easy.

- C. Having studied for weeks, I found the exam easy.

- D. Having studied for weeks, the exam felt easy.

**Answer:** C. The modifier attaches to "I," the one who studied. D and A attach studying to the exam; B is wordy and still leaves the studier unclear.

## Q11 — Subject-Verb (Easy)

**Passage:** The dogs in the park barks loudly every morning.

**Question:** Which choice is correct?

- A. The dogs in the park barking loudly every morning.

- B. The park dogs barks loudly every morning.

- C. The dogs in the park bark loudly every morning.

- D. The dogs in the park barks loudly every morning.

**Answer:** C. Plural "dogs" needs "bark." D keeps the singular verb; A is a fragment; B repeats the agreement error.

## Q12 — Parallelism (Medium)

**Passage:** The course requires attending lectures, to complete labs, and a final project.

**Question:** Which choice is parallel?

- A. The course requires to attend lectures, completing labs, and a final project.

- B. The course requires attendance, to complete labs, and project.

- C. The course requires attending lectures, completing labs, and finishing a final project.

- D. The course requires attending lectures, to complete labs, and a final project.

**Answer:** C. Three parallel gerunds. D–B mix forms.

## Q13 — Pronoun Clarity (Medium)

**Passage:** Maya told Zoe that she had won the scholarship.

**Question:** If Maya won, which choice is clearest?

- A. Maya told Zoe that they had won the scholarship.

- B. Maya told Zoe that it had won the scholarship.

- C. Maya told Zoe that she, Maya, had won the scholarship.

- D. Maya told Zoe that she had won the scholarship.

**Answer:** C. Names the winner explicitly. D stays ambiguous; A and B mismatch number and sense.

## Q14 — Comma Splice (Medium)

**Passage:** The storm knocked out power, the hospital switched to generators.

**Question:** Which choice is correct?

- A. The storm knocked out power the hospital switched to generators.

- B. The storm knocked out power; because the hospital switched.

- C. The storm knocked out power, so the hospital switched to generators.

- D. The storm knocked out power, the hospital switched to generators.

**Answer:** C. Comma + "so" joins the clauses. D is the original splice; A has no boundary; B pairs a semicolon with a fragment.

## Q15 — Modifier (Hard)

**Passage:** Covered in mud, the kids carried the puppy inside.

**Question:** Who was covered in mud? If the puppy was, which choice fixes it?

- A. The kids, covered in mud, carried inside the puppy mud.

- B. Covered in mud the kids carried the puppy inside.

- C. The kids carried the mud-covered puppy inside.

- D. Covered in mud, the kids carried the puppy inside.

**Answer:** C. Places "mud-covered" directly on the puppy. D attaches mud to the kids; A is garbled; B drops the needed comma.

## Q16 — Verb Tense (Easy)

**Passage:** Yesterday she walks to work even though it rained.

**Question:** Which choice is correct?

- A. Yesterday she will walk to work even though it rained.

- B. Yesterday she walking to work even though it rained.

- C. Yesterday she walked to work even though it rained.

- D. Yesterday she walks to work even though it rained.

**Answer:** C. "Yesterday" requires past tense. D is present; A is future; B is a fragment.

## Q17 — Apostrophes (Medium)

**Passage:** The teachers lounge is next to the students lockers.

**Question:** Which choice is correct?

- A. The teacher's lounge is next to the student's lockers.

- B. The teachers's lounge is next to the students's lockers.

- C. The teachers' lounge is next to the students' lockers.

- D. The teachers lounge is next to the students lockers.

**Answer:** C. Plural possessives for both groups. D has no possession; A wrongly singularizes; B doubles the s incorrectly.

## Q18 — Parallelism (Hard)

**Passage:** Good managers listen carefully, speak honestly, and with fairness act.

**Question:** Which choice is parallel?

- A. Good managers listen carefully, speak honestly, and with fairness act.

- B. Good managers listen carefully, honest speaking, and fairness acting.

- C. Good managers listening carefully, speak honestly, and act fairly.

- D. Good managers listen carefully, speak honestly, and act fairly.

**Answer:** D. Three parallel verb + adverb pairs. A inverts the third; B mixes nouns; C mixes participle with verbs.

## Q19 — Subject-Verb Distance (Medium)

**Passage:** The box of donated books were delivered on Tuesday.

**Question:** Which choice is correct?

- A. The box of donated books were delivered on Tuesday.

- B. The box of donated books were being delivered on Tuesday.

- C. The box of donated books delivering on Tuesday.

- D. The box of donated books was delivered on Tuesday.

**Answer:** D. Subject "box" is singular. A agrees with "books"; B keeps plural with awkward tense; C is a fragment.

## Q20 — Colon Use (Medium)

**Passage:** Bring these items: a tent, a lantern, and warm clothes.

**Question:** Which choice is correct?

- A. Bring these items; a tent, a lantern, and warm clothes.

- B. Bring: these items a tent, a lantern, and warm clothes.

- C. Bring these items, a tent, a lantern, and warm clothes.

- D. Bring these items: a tent, a lantern, and warm clothes. (No change)

**Answer:** D. Colon after a complete clause introduces a list. A misuses a semicolon; B splits subject and verb; C undersells the list boundary.

## Q21 — Dangling Participle (Medium)

**Passage:** Exhausted from the hike, the cabin looked welcoming.

**Question:** Which choice fixes the error?

- A. Exhausted from the hike, the cabin looked welcoming.

- B. The cabin, exhausted from the hike, looked welcoming.

- C. Exhausted, from the hike the cabin looked welcoming.

- D. Exhausted from the hike, we found the cabin welcoming.

**Answer:** D. Names "we" as the exhausted hikers. A and B make the cabin exhausted; C keeps the dangle with worse punctuation.

## Q22 — Pronoun Number (Easy)

**Passage:** Every player must bring their own water bottle.

**Question:** In formal writing, which choice is best?

- A. Every player must bring their own water bottle.

- B. Every player must bring our own water bottle.

- C. Every player must bring them own water bottle.

- D. Every player must bring his or her own water bottle.

**Answer:** D. Singular agreement with "every player." A is common but informal here; B shifts person; C is ungrammatical.

## Q23 — Semicolon (Hard)

**Passage:** The results were clear; the treatment worked, the control group unchanged.

**Question:** Which choice correctly punctuates the second half?

- A. The results were clear; the treatment worked, the control group unchanged.

- B. The results were clear, the treatment worked, the control group unchanged.

- C. The results were clear the treatment worked the control group unchanged.

- D. The results were clear: the treatment worked while the control group stayed unchanged.

**Answer:** D. Colon introduces the explanation with a complete clause. A leaves a fragment after the comma; B is a splice chain; C has no boundaries.

## Q24 — Tense Consistency (Medium)

**Passage:** She opens the letter, reads it twice, and then filed it away.

**Question:** Which choice is consistent?

- A. She opens the letter, reads it twice, and then filed it away.

- B. She opens the letter, read it twice, and then files it away.

- C. She opened the letter, reads it twice, and then filing it away.

- D. She opened the letter, read it twice, and then filed it away.

**Answer:** D. All past tense. A–C mix present and past.

## Q25 — Restrictive Clause (Medium)

**Passage:** Students, who finish early, may leave quietly.

**Question:** If the permission applies ONLY to early finishers, which choice is correct?

- A. Students, who finish early, may leave quietly.

- B. Students; who finish early may leave quietly.

- C. Students who finish early, may leave quietly.

- D. Students who finish early may leave quietly.

**Answer:** D. No commas = restrictive (only early finishers). A's commas extend permission to all students; B misuses a semicolon; C adds a stray comma.

## Q26 — Wordiness (Easy)

**Passage:** Due to the fact that it rained, the game was canceled.

**Question:** Which choice is most concise?

- A. Because it rained, the game was canceled.

- B. Due to the fact that it rained, the game was canceled.

- C. Because of the fact that raining occurred, cancellation happened.

- D. Rain being the thing that was falling, the game got canceled.

**Answer:** A. "Because" replaces five words cleanly. B is the original bloat; C and D are worse.

## Q27 — Agreement: Neither/Nor (Hard)

**Passage:** Neither the coaches nor the captain were informed of the schedule change.

**Question:** Which choice is correct? ("Captain" is closest to the verb.)

- A. Neither the coaches nor the captain was informed of the schedule change.

- B. Neither the coaches nor the captain were informed of the schedule change.

- C. Neither the coaches nor the captain be informed of the schedule change.

- D. Neither the coaches nor the captain informing of the schedule change.

**Answer:** A. Proximity rule: verb agrees with "captain" (singular). B agrees with the farther noun; C and D are ungrammatical.

## Q28 — Comma With FANBOYS (Easy)

**Passage:** She studied hard for the test but still felt nervous.

**Question:** Which choice is correct?

- A. She studied hard for the test, but she still felt nervous.

- B. She studied hard for the test but still felt nervous. (No change)

- C. She studied hard for the test, but still felt nervous.

- D. She studied hard for the test; but still felt nervous.

**Answer:** B. No comma needed: "but" joins a verb phrase, not two independent clauses (no second subject). A adds an unnecessary subject+comma; C adds a comma without a subject; D misuses a semicolon.

## Q29 — Misplaced Modifier (Medium)

**Passage:** She served sandwiches to the kids on paper plates.

**Question:** If the sandwiches (not the kids) are on paper plates, which choice is clearest?

- A. She served sandwiches on paper plates to the kids.

- B. She served sandwiches to the kids on paper plates.

- C. On paper plates she served sandwiches to the kids on them.

- D. She served to the kids on paper plates sandwiches.

**Answer:** A. Keeps "on paper plates" beside "sandwiches." B suggests kids on plates; C and D garble placement.

## Q30 — Its/It's (Easy)

**Passage:** The dog wagged it's tail when it's owner arrived.

**Question:** Which choice is correct?

- A. The dog wagged its tail when its owner arrived.

- B. The dog wagged it's tail when it's owner arrived.

- C. The dog wagged its tail when it's owner arrived.

- D. The dog wagged it's tail when its owner arrived.

**Answer:** A. Possessive "its" both times. B–D misuse the contraction "it's" (= it is).

## Q31 — Parallel Comparison (Hard)

**Passage:** The new route is faster, cheaper, and more safe than the old one.

**Question:** Which choice is parallel and idiomatic?

- A. The new route is faster, cheaper, and safer than the old one.

- B. The new route is faster, cheaper, and more safe than the old one.

- C. The new route is fast, cheaper, and safest than the old one.

- D. The new route is faster, cheap, and safety than the old one.

**Answer:** A. Three parallel comparatives. B breaks parallelism with "more safe"; C mixes positive with comparatives; D mixes nouns and adjectives.

## Q32 — Run-on Fix (Medium)

**Passage:** The elevator broke we took the stairs.

**Question:** Which choice is correct?

- A. The elevator broke, so we took the stairs.

- B. The elevator broke we took the stairs.

- C. The elevator broke, we took the stairs.

- D. The elevator broke; so we took the stairs.

**Answer:** A. Comma + "so" fixes the run-on. B is unchanged; C is a splice; D misuses a semicolon before "so."

## Q33 — Who/Whom (Medium)

**Passage:** The manager, who the staff respects, approved the plan.

**Question:** In formal writing ("the staff respects him"), which choice is correct?

- A. The manager, whom the staff respects, approved the plan.

- B. The manager, who the staff respects, approved the plan.

- C. The manager, which the staff respects, approved the plan.

- D. The manager, whose the staff respects, approved the plan.

**Answer:** A. Object of "respects" → "whom." B uses subject form; C uses "which" for a person; D misuses "whose."

## Q34 — Double Negative (Easy)

**Passage:** She didn't have no reason to leave early.

**Question:** Which choice is correct?

- A. She had no reason to leave early.

- B. She didn't have no reason to leave early.

- C. She didn't have any no reason to leave early.

- D. She hadn't no reason to leave early never.

**Answer:** A. Single negative, clean. B–D stack negatives.

## Q35 — Logical Comparison (Hard)

**Passage:** The rivers in the north are cleaner than the south.

**Question:** Which choice completes the comparison logically?

- A. The rivers in the north are cleaner than those in the south.

- B. The rivers in the north are cleaner than the south.

- C. The rivers in the north are cleaner than southern.

- D. The rivers in the north are cleaner than in the south rivers.

**Answer:** A. Rivers compared with rivers ("those"). B compares rivers with a region; C and D are unidiomatic fragments.
