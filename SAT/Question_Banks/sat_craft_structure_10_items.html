Craft & Structure — 35 Practice Items

Q1 — Main Purpose (Easy)

Passage: A study of urban bees found that gardens with native wildflowers increased pollinator visits by 40% compared to lawns.

Question: The passage primarily serves to…

  • A. criticize traditional landscaping practices.
  • B. persuade readers to replace lawns with wildflowers.
  • C. report a specific research finding about pollinator behavior.
  • D. compare urban and rural pollinator populations.
Answer: C. The passage states a research result, not a prescription (B), not a comparison (D), not a critique (A).

Q2 — Transition / Function (Medium)

Passage: "Although solar costs have fallen, grid storage remains the bottleneck. Nevertheless, recent battery chemistries suggest a path forward."

Question: The phrase "Nevertheless, recent battery chemistries..." primarily functions to…

  • A. define "grid storage."
  • B. summarize the passage’s main conclusion.
  • C. concede a limitation then introduce a solution.
  • D. contrast solar with wind energy.
Answer: C. "Although... bottleneck" = concession; "Nevertheless..." = pivot to solution.

Q3 — Word in Context (Medium)

Passage: Ecosystems that lost key predator species often became fragile, not resilient, after invasive plants arrived.

Question: In this context, "resilient" most nearly means…

  • A. resistant to all change.
  • B. easily repaired by human intervention.
  • C. able to recover from disturbances.
  • D. permanently altered.
Answer: C. Resilient = able to recover; opposite of fragile.

Q4 — Evidence / Inference (Hard)

Passage: A historian argues that 19th-century labor strikes were less about wages and more about time autonomy. Supporting evidence: strike demands increasingly mentioned fixed-hour clauses over pay increases after 1870.

Question: Which finding, if true, would best support the historian’s claim?

  • A. Strike frequency fell after 1870.
  • B. Wages rose faster after 1870 than before.
  • C. Strike demands after 1870 emphasized scheduling over salary in 78% of cases.
  • D. Factory owners reduced hours voluntarily.
Answer: C. Direct alignment with claim; others are irrelevant or contradictory.

Q5 — Main Purpose (Medium)

Passage: This brief proposes a graduated carbon fee on industrial emissions paired with rebates for low-income households.

Question: The passage primarily serves to…

  • A. compare household income levels.
  • B. describe the history of carbon pricing.
  • C. propose a specific policy mechanism.
  • D. argue that carbon fees always fail.
Answer: C. Explicit proposal; not history (B), not failure argument (D), not comparison (A).

Q6 — Function of Paragraph (Medium)

Passage: Paragraph 1 claims open-plan offices increase collaboration. Paragraph 2 notes that noise-related distractions reduced deep-work output by 30% in one study.

Question: The function of Paragraph 2 is to…

  • A. summarize the passage.
  • B. provide additional supporting evidence.
  • C. qualify/limit the claim in Paragraph 1.
  • D. introduce a new unrelated topic.
Answer: C. Counter-evidence limits the initial claim.

Q7 — Inference (Hard)

Passage: A study of 500 readers found that those who annotated texts with margin questions retained 25% more main ideas than passive readers.

Question: Which conclusion is best supported?

  • A. Passive reading is never useful.
  • B. All readers should annotate.
  • C. Active engagement improves retention.
  • D. Margin questions are the only effective method.
Answer: C. Supported by data; others overgeneralize (B, D, A).

Q8 — Word in Context (Medium)

Passage: The economic downturn had profound effects on supply chains, altering inventory strategies for years.

Question: "Profound" most nearly means…

  • A. temporary.
  • B. mild.
  • C. far-reaching / deep.
  • D. unexpected only.
Answer: C. Context: long-lasting alteration = deep/far-reaching.

Q9 — Evidence Pairing (Hard)

Passage: Author claims that local election turnout rose because of community-led registration drives. Evidence options provided.

Question: Which two sentences best support the claim?

  • A. Local drives registered 3,000 voters; turnout rose 12%.
  • B. Media coverage increased; weather was good.
  • C. Older voters turned out more; registration was flat.
  • D. Turnout rose statewide; local drives stalled.
Answer: A. Direct correlation between drive and turnout; others are unrelated or contradictory.

Q10 — Main Purpose (Medium)

Passage: A scientific article explains how cloud-albedo feedback loops amplify temperature changes in climate models.

Question: The passage primarily serves to…

  • A. predict future temperatures exactly.
  • B. criticize climate models.
  • C. describe a mechanism.
  • D. compare cloud types.
Answer: C. Explains mechanism; not critique (B), not comparison (D), not exact prediction (A).

Q11 — Main Purpose (Easy)

Passage: Marine biologists found that coral fragments grown on underwater frames survived at twice the rate of those planted directly on damaged reefs.

Question: The passage primarily serves to…

  • A. compare oceans and freshwater lakes.
  • B. criticize marine biologists.
  • C. argue that all reefs can be fully restored.
  • D. report a finding about coral restoration methods.
Answer: D. It reports a comparative survival finding. C overstates ("all... fully"); A introduces an unmentioned comparison; B contradicts the neutral tone.

Q12 — Function of Example (Medium)

Passage: "Cities waste enormous energy lighting empty streets. In Oslo, motion-sensitive lamps cut lighting costs by 40%."

Question: The Oslo sentence primarily functions to…

  • A. define motion-sensitive lamps.
  • B. compare Oslo with rural towns.
  • C. state the passage's main conclusion.
  • D. illustrate the claim with a concrete case.
Answer: D. It exemplifies the waste-reduction claim. C is too grand for one sentence; A gives no definition; B makes no rural comparison.

Q13 — Word in Context (Easy)

Passage: After the reforms, the small clinic remained a robust provider of rural care despite funding pressure.

Question: In this context, "robust" most nearly means…

  • A. newly built.
  • B. temporarily open.
  • C. loud and noisy.
  • D. strong and healthy.
Answer: D. The clinic endures pressure, so "strong/healthy" fits. C confuses senses; A and B contradict "remained."

Q14 — Cross-Text Agreement (Medium)

Passage: Text 1 argues congestion pricing reduces downtown traffic. Text 2 reports that after pricing began, bus ridership rose 18% while car entries fell 22%.

Question: How would the author of Text 2 most likely respond to Text 1's claim?

  • A. By arguing pricing harms bus riders.
  • B. By comparing pricing with parking fines.
  • C. By rejecting it as unsupported.
  • D. By supporting it with ridership and traffic data.
Answer: D. The data aligns with the claim. C contradicts the supportive numbers; A invents harm; B introduces an unmentioned comparison.

Q15 — Purpose: Proposal (Medium)

Passage: This memo proposes staggered school start times — high schools at 8:45, elementary at 8:00 — to match adolescent sleep cycles and ease bus congestion.

Question: The passage primarily serves to…

  • A. prove teenagers need no sleep.
  • B. compare buses with trains.
  • C. narrate the history of school schedules.
  • D. propose a specific scheduling change and its reasons.
Answer: D. It proposes times plus two reasons. C finds no history; A inverts the sleep point; B adds an absent comparison.

Q16 — Concession Function (Hard)

Passage: "Admittedly, the trial enrolled only 90 patients. Yet its double-blind design and consistent dosing make the results difficult to dismiss."

Question: The first sentence primarily functions to…

  • A. describe the dosing procedure.
  • B. criticize double-blind designs.
  • C. summarize the trial's conclusion.
  • D. concede a limitation before defending the findings.
Answer: D. "Admittedly" concedes size; "yet" pivots to defense. C mistakes concession for conclusion; A and B misread the content.

Q17 — Word in Context (Medium)

Passage: The contrast between the two neighborhoods was stark: one had tree-lined streets and a library; the other had neither.

Question: In this context, "stark" most nearly means…

  • A. gradual and subtle.
  • B. temporary and fading.
  • C. dark and frightening.
  • D. sharp and clear.
Answer: D. The listed differences are sharp and obvious. C adds fear; A is the opposite; B adds time limits absent from the text.

Q18 — Author Stance (Medium)

Passage: The reviewer calls the novel "ambitious but uneven," praising its structure while noting that the final chapters rush toward resolution.

Question: The reviewer's stance is best described as…

  • A. entirely dismissive.
  • B. focused only on sales figures.
  • C. unreservedly enthusiastic.
  • D. qualified appreciation with a specific criticism.
Answer: D. Praise plus a named flaw. C ignores the criticism; A ignores the praise; B invents a sales focus.

Q19 — Purpose: Qualification (Hard)

Passage: Popular accounts credit a single inventor with the telephone, but patent records show a crowded race in which several rivals filed overlapping claims within months.

Question: The passage primarily serves to…

  • A. qualify a popular account with complicating evidence.
  • B. explain how telephones transmit sound.
  • C. list every telephone patent ever filed.
  • D. celebrate a lone genius.
Answer: A. "But" plus patent records complicates the simple story. D is what the passage pushes against; B explains mechanism, not history; C overstates scope.

Q20 — Function of Definition (Easy)

Passage: "Photosynthesis — the process by which plants convert sunlight into chemical energy — powers nearly every food chain."

Question: The dashes primarily serve to…

  • A. insert a definition of the key term.
  • B. ask the reader a question.
  • C. compare plants with animals.
  • D. conclude the passage.
Answer: A. The dashed phrase defines photosynthesis. B finds no question; C makes no comparison; D mistakes an opener for a conclusion.

Q21 — Word in Context (Hard)

Passage: The scientist gave qualified support to the theory, endorsing its mathematics while questioning its assumptions about initial conditions.

Question: In this context, "qualified" most nearly means…

  • A. limited or conditional.
  • B. highly enthusiastic.
  • C. recently added.
  • D. certified by an authority.
Answer: A. Endorsing one part while questioning another = conditional support. D confuses credential senses; B contradicts the questioning; C invents recency.

Q22 — Cross-Text Disagreement (Hard)

Passage: Text 1 claims homework improves achievement at all grade levels. Text 2 cites studies finding no achievement gains from homework in elementary grades.

Question: The relationship between the texts is best described as…

  • A. Text 2 provides evidence that challenges Text 1's universal claim.
  • B. Text 2 restates Text 1 in different words.
  • C. Text 2 describes Text 1's methodology.
  • D. The texts address unrelated topics.
Answer: A. "At all grade levels" versus "no gains in elementary" is a direct challenge. B misses the conflict; C invents methodology discussion; D ignores the shared topic.

Q23 — Purpose: Explanation (Easy)

Passage: Leaves appear green because chlorophyll absorbs red and blue light while reflecting green wavelengths back to our eyes.

Question: The passage primarily serves to…

  • A. persuade gardeners to plant more trees.
  • B. explain why leaves look green.
  • C. compare leaves with flowers.
  • D. criticize botany textbooks.
Answer: B. It explains the mechanism behind the color. A adds persuasion; C adds comparison; D adds criticism — none present.

Q24 — Rhetorical Question Function (Medium)

Passage: "If the dam produces cheap power, why do downstream towns pay the highest rates? The answer lies in transmission contracts signed decades ago."

Question: The opening question primarily functions to…

  • A. request information the author lacks.
  • B. highlight a puzzle the passage will resolve.
  • C. prove dams are useless.
  • D. survey readers about their bills.
Answer: B. The next sentence promises the answer. A mistakes rhetoric for genuine inquiry; C overstates; D invents a survey.

Q25 — Word in Context (Medium)

Passage: The lawyer's most compelling argument was not emotional appeal but a timeline showing the defendant was miles away at the time.

Question: In this context, "compelling" most nearly means…

  • A. forceful or persuasive.
  • B. legally required.
  • C. complicated and confusing.
  • D. briefly mentioned.
Answer: A. The timeline persuades, so "persuasive" fits. B confuses senses; C contradicts clarity; D contradicts "most."

Q26 — Purpose: Illustration of Trait (Medium)

Passage: As a young clerk, the future mayor walked six miles each way to deliver petitions, a habit neighbors cited for decades as proof of her persistence.

Question: The anecdote primarily serves to…

  • A. list every petition she delivered.
  • B. illustrate the trait of persistence with a concrete episode.
  • C. argue walking is the best exercise.
  • D. compare clerks with mayors.
Answer: B. One episode exemplifies persistence. A demands an exhaustive list; C moralizes about exercise; D compares roles never contrasted.

Q27 — Counterargument Function (Hard)

Passage: "Critics warn the reservoir will displace farms. Those concerns undervalue the irrigation the dam guarantees those same farms in drought years."

Question: The second sentence primarily functions to…

  • A. concede the critics are entirely right.
  • B. answer the objection by citing an offsetting benefit.
  • C. change the subject to drought history.
  • D. define irrigation for readers.
Answer: B. It rebuts displacement fears with guaranteed irrigation. A surrenders instead of rebutting; C mistakes rebuttal for digression; D finds no definition.

Q28 — Word in Context (Easy)

Passage: Tourism declined sharply after the ferry route was suspended, and hotels reported a 30% drop in bookings.

Question: In this context, "declined" most nearly means…

  • A. refused politely.
  • B. decreased.
  • C. improved slowly.
  • D. remained steady.
Answer: B. Bookings fell 30%, so "decreased" fits. A uses the refusal sense wrongly; C and D contradict the drop.

Q29 — Text 2 Responds (Medium)

Passage: Text 1 presents a new 571-page biography as the definitive life of the artist. Text 2 notes the biography omits the artist's final decade entirely.

Question: Text 2 most likely responds to Text 1 by…

  • A. endorsing the "definitive" description.
  • B. questioning completeness given the missing decade.
  • C. summarizing all 571 pages.
  • D. comparing the artist with other painters.
Answer: B. Omitting ten years challenges "definitive." A ignores the omission; C claims a full summary; D adds painters never mentioned.

Q30 — Purpose: Review Article (Hard)

Passage: This review surveys a decade of sleep research, weighs conflicting findings on naps, and identifies chronotype studies as the most promising direction.

Question: The passage primarily serves to…

  • A. report one new experiment.
  • B. synthesize a field and point to future work.
  • C. instruct readers how to fall asleep.
  • D. dismiss all prior sleep research.
Answer: B. Survey + weigh + "most promising direction" = synthesis with outlook. A narrows to one study; C turns review into advice; D contradicts the weighing tone.

Q31 — Definition Function (Easy)

Passage: "A tariff is a tax on imported goods. When tariffs rise, importers typically pass the cost to shoppers."

Question: The first sentence primarily functions to…

  • A. define the key term for what follows.
  • B. argue tariffs should be abolished.
  • C. compare taxes across countries.
  • D. tell a story about shoppers.
Answer: A. It defines "tariff" before explaining effects. B adds argument; C adds countries; D adds narrative — all absent.

Q32 — Word in Context (Medium)

Passage: The engineer proposed a novel cooling method that uses no water, unlike the plant's current system.

Question: In this context, "novel" most nearly means…

  • A. book-length.
  • B. new or original.
  • C. complicated.
  • D. expensive.
Answer: B. Contrast with "current system" signals newness. A uses the noun sense; C and D add traits never stated.

Q33 — Purpose: Evocation (Medium)

Passage: Dawn over the harbor: gulls crying, nets heavy with silver fish, diesel and salt on the cold air.

Question: The passage primarily serves to…

  • A. present catch statistics.
  • B. evoke a sensory scene.
  • C. argue for fishing quotas.
  • D. explain diesel engines.
Answer: B. Imagery of sound, sight, and smell creates atmosphere. A wants numbers; C wants policy; D wants mechanics — none present.

Q34 — Role of Statistic (Medium)

Passage: "Recycling works: the town diverted 62% of waste from landfill within two years of starting curbside pickup."

Question: The statistic "62%" primarily functions to…

  • A. prove the opening claim with measurable evidence.
  • B. define the word recycling.
  • C. predict future landfill capacity exactly.
  • D. compare two towns.
Answer: A. The number evidences "recycling works." B finds no definition; C demands exact prediction; D needs a second town.

Q35 — Synthesis of Purpose (Hard)

Passage: The essay opens by praising a bridge's elegance, then details the political fights that delayed it for a decade, closing with the claim that infrastructure is never only engineering.

Question: Which choice best describes the passage's overall structure?

  • A. Admiration, complicating history, broader claim.
  • B. Chronological biography of the architect.
  • C. Step-by-step construction manual.
  • D. Argument that bridges should not be built.
Answer: A. It tracks praise → political history → general claim. B invents a biography; C invents instructions; D inverts the admiring tone.
