Q1 — Stated Finding (Easy)

Passage: A study of 200 students found that those who regularly slept eight or more hours per night scored an average of 15 points higher on final exams than students who slept seven or fewer hours.

Question: According to the text, what did the study find?

  • A. Only 200 students in total sleep eight or more hours per night.
  • B. Students who slept eight or more hours scored higher on average than those who slept less.
  • C. All students who slept less than eight hours failed their final exams.
  • D. Sleep has no measurable effect on exam performance.
Answer: B. The text states the 15-point average difference directly. C overstates ("all... failed"); D contradicts the finding; A misreads the sample size as a population claim.

Q2 — Data Interpretation (Medium)

Passage: Annual bookstore sales (in thousands) were: 2019: 100, 2020: 120, 2021: 140, 2022: 160, 2023: 180.

Question: Which choice most effectively uses data from the table to support the claim that sales grew steadily?

  • A. Sales doubled between 2020 and 2021.
  • B. Sales increased every year, rising from 100 in 2019 to 180 in 2023.
  • C. Sales were flat between 2021 and 2023.
  • D. Sales fell from 180 to 100 over the period.
Answer: B. Every yearly value rises and endpoints match. C contradicts the rising values; D reverses the trend; A misstates 120 to 140 as doubling.

Q3 — Inference (Medium)

Passage: After the city invested in protected cycling lanes, the share of commuters cycling to work rose from 3% to 8% within three years, while driving shares declined.

Question: Which choice is best supported by the text?

  • A. Driving shares increased after the investment.
  • B. The new cycling lanes were correlated with a shift toward cycling commutes.
  • C. Cycling lanes caused every driver to switch to cycling.
  • D. The investment had no effect on commuting patterns.
Answer: B. The text supports correlation with mode shift. C overstates ("every driver"); D and A contradict the stated changes.

Q4 — Central Claim (Medium)

Passage: Remote work can raise productivity, but only when teams adopt clear communication norms: shared response-time expectations, written decision logs, and regular check-ins. Without these, remote teams report more delays than office teams.

Question: Which choice best states the main claim?

  • A. Office teams never experience delays.
  • B. Remote work raises productivity when paired with clear communication norms.
  • C. Remote work always outperforms office work.
  • D. Communication norms are unnecessary for remote teams.
Answer: B. Captures the conditional claim exactly. C drops the condition; D contradicts the passage; A is an unsupported absolute.

Q5 — Supporting Evidence (Hard)

Passage: A researcher claims that recent budget cuts harmed rural clinics more severely than urban ones.

Question: Which finding, if true, would most directly support the researcher's claim?

  • A. Rural clinic visits declined while funding stayed flat.
  • B. Rural clinics lost 30% of funding and cut 25% of staff, while urban clinics lost 5% of funding and 3% of staff.
  • C. Both rural and urban clinics received national awards last year.
  • D. Urban clinics hired more specialists than rural clinics did.
Answer: B. Directly compares harm across both groups with magnitude. C is irrelevant praise; D lacks a before/after comparison; A shows a decline without the urban comparison or a funding cut.

Q6 — Quantitative Comparison (Hard)

Passage: A combined heat index (temperature score + rainfall score) was computed for four years: 2020: 12 + 9 = 21; 2021: 15 + 8 = 23; 2022: 14 + 12 = 26; 2023: 16 + 7 = 23.

Question: Based on the data, which year had the highest combined index?

  • A. 2020
  • B. 2021
  • C. 2022
  • D. 2023
Answer: C. 14 + 12 = 26 exceeds 21, 23, and 23. A–B and D pick years with lower totals, confusing a single high component for the combined total.

Q7 — Inference (Medium)

Passage: Despite introducing free entry, a museum saw attendance drop 20%. Visitor surveys cited lack of awareness of the policy — not ticket cost — as the main reason for not visiting.

Question: Which conclusion is best supported by the text?

  • A. Surveys are unreliable measures of visitor opinion.
  • B. Improving public awareness of free entry might raise attendance.
  • C. Ticket cost was the main barrier to attendance.
  • D. Free entry caused attendance to fall.
Answer: B. Follows directly from the survey result. C contradicts the surveys; D confuses correlation with causation; A is an unsupported generalization.

Q8 — Textual Evidence (Hard)

Passage: A psychologist claims bilingual education strengthens executive function. In one study, bilingual children outperformed monolingual peers on task-switching tests by 18%, while performing equally on vocabulary tests.

Question: Which quotation from the study most effectively illustrates the psychologist's claim?

  • A. Monolingual children preferred vocabulary tasks.
  • B. Bilingual children outperformed monolingual peers on task-switching tests by 18%.
  • C. Both groups performed equally on vocabulary tests.
  • D. The study enrolled children from twelve schools.
Answer: B. Task-switching measures executive function directly. C shows a non-difference; D describes method only; A reports a preference, not performance.

Q9 — Trend Description (Medium)

Passage: Library readership (in thousands): 2019: 12, 2020: 15, 2021: 20. The largest single-year gain occurred between 2020 and 2021.

Question: Which choice accurately describes the data?

  • A. Readership was flat across all three years.
  • B. The greatest gain occurred between 2019 and 2020.
  • C. Readership grew each year, with the greatest gain from 2020 to 2021.
  • D. Readership declined after 2020.
Answer: C. Values rise yearly (12→15→20); gains are 3 then 5. D and A contradict the rising values; B picks the smaller gain (3 < 5).

Q10 — Central Idea (Easy)

Passage: Cities are converting abandoned factories into affordable housing. This adaptive reuse preserves historic structures, reduces construction waste, and adds homes without new sprawl.

Question: Which choice best states the main idea?

  • A. Historic factories must be demolished for safety.
  • B. Construction waste cannot be reduced.
  • C. Repurposing old factories as housing supports sustainability and adds homes.
  • D. All new housing should be built on undeveloped land.
Answer: C. Summarizes reuse benefits stated in the text. D contradicts "without new sprawl"; A and B contradict the passage directly.

Q11 — Stated Detail (Easy)

Passage: Honeybees communicate the location of flowers through a "waggle dance" whose angle indicates direction and whose duration indicates distance.

Question: According to the text, what does the dance's duration indicate?

  • A. The number of bees in the hive.
  • B. The season of the year.
  • C. Distance to the flowers.
  • D. Direction of the flowers.
Answer: C. Duration = distance; angle = direction. D swaps the two; A and B are never mentioned.

Q12 — Data Trend (Medium)

Passage: Average commute times (minutes): 2018: 26, 2019: 27, 2020: 22, 2021: 25, 2022: 28.

Question: Which statement is best supported by the data?

  • A. 2022 had the shortest commute on record.
  • B. Commutes never changed.
  • C. Commutes fell sharply in 2020, then rose again through 2022.
  • D. Commutes rose every single year.
Answer: C. 27→22 is the dip; 22→25→28 is the climb. D ignores the 2020 drop; A picks the longest year; B denies all change.

Q13 — Inference (Medium)

Passage: The new reservoir filled two months ahead of schedule after an unusually wet spring, allowing farmers to irrigate early.

Question: Which choice is best supported?

  • A. Reservoirs always fill ahead of schedule.
  • B. Irrigation delayed the filling.
  • C. Wetter-than-usual weather likely accelerated the filling.
  • D. Farmers caused the wet spring.
Answer: C. The text links the wet spring to early filling. D reverses causation; A generalizes from one case; B contradicts "allowing farmers to irrigate early."

Q14 — Central Claim (Medium)

Passage: Urban tree cover lowers summer temperatures, filters air pollution, and raises property values — yet most cities plant fewer trees each year than they remove.

Question: Which choice best states the main claim?

  • A. Property values always fall near parks.
  • B. Air pollution cannot be filtered.
  • C. Cities should plant more trees given their proven benefits.
  • D. Trees have no effect on city life.
Answer: C. Benefits listed + removal trend = implied call to plant. D–B all contradict stated benefits.

Q15 — Undermine (Hard)

Passage: A startup claims its app doubled users' vocabulary in a month.

Question: Which finding, if true, would most directly undermine the claim?

  • A. Vocabulary tests were administered after one month.
  • B. Some users enjoyed the app's colors.
  • C. Users studied with the app for only two days on average.
  • D. The app received a design award.
Answer: C. Two days of use cannot support a month-long doubling claim. D and B are irrelevant praise; A describes consistent method.

Q16 — Quantitative Share (Medium)

Passage: A school of 800 students: 320 walk, 240 take the bus, 160 cycle, 80 are driven.

Question: What share of students walk?

  • A. 25%
  • B. 50%
  • C. 40%
  • D. 32%
Answer: C. 320/800 = 40%. D uses 320/1000; A is the cycling share; B halves the school.

Q17 — Textual Evidence (Hard)

Passage: An architect claims courtyards cool buildings passively. Measurements show courtyard rooms stayed 4°C cooler than street-facing rooms during a heatwave, with no air conditioning running.

Question: Which quotation most effectively illustrates the claim?

  • A. The building has two courtyards.
  • B. Air conditioning was installed last year.
  • C. Street-facing rooms have larger windows.
  • D. Courtyard rooms stayed 4°C cooler than street-facing rooms with no air conditioning.
Answer: D. Direct cooling comparison under passive conditions. A is mere description; B contradicts "no air conditioning"; C is an unrelated detail.

Q18 — Inference (Easy)

Passage: Attendance at weekend farmers' markets tripled after vendors began accepting card payments.

Question: Which conclusion is best supported?

  • A. Produce prices tripled.
  • B. Weekend markets sell only cards.
  • C. Cash payments increased.
  • D. Easier payment likely encouraged more visits.
Answer: D. The timing supports the payment explanation. A confuses attendance with prices; B is nonsensical; C contradicts the card shift.

Q19 — Claim vs Detail (Medium)

Passage: The mayor announced a "historic" drop in crime, citing a 2% decline concentrated in a single district while citywide numbers were flat.

Question: Which choice best describes the announcement?

  • A. It accurately reports a historic citywide transformation.
  • B. It denies that any data was collected.
  • C. It compares crime across different decades.
  • D. It overstates a small, localized change as historic.
Answer: D. 2% in one district with flat citywide = overstatement. A accepts the spin; B denies data that exists; C invents decades.

Q20 — Data Comparison (Hard)

Passage: Two fertilizers tested on identical plots: Plot A yielded 40 bushels, Plot B yielded 52 bushels. Rainfall and soil were equal.

Question: Which conclusion is best supported?

  • A. Rainfall caused the difference.
  • B. Both plots yielded identically.
  • C. Soil differences explain the gap.
  • D. Fertilizer B outperformed A under controlled conditions.
Answer: D. Equal conditions isolate fertilizer as the cause. A and C contradict "equal"; B denies the 40 vs 52 gap.

Q21 — Stated Cause (Easy)

Passage: The library extended evening hours after surveys showed most patrons visit after work.

Question: Why did the library extend its hours?

  • A. Morning visits exceeded capacity.
  • B. The building needed repairs.
  • C. Staff requested shorter shifts.
  • D. Surveys showed patrons mostly visit after work.
Answer: D. Stated directly. A invents a morning problem; B and C are never mentioned.

Q22 — Trend Exception (Medium)

Passage: Screen time rose each year from 2015 to 2022 except in 2020, when outdoor activity spiked during lockdowns.

Question: Which choice is accurate?

  • A. Screen time fell every year.
  • B. 2020 had the highest screen time.
  • C. Outdoor activity never changed.
  • D. Screen time rose yearly except for 2020.
Answer: D. Matches "each year... except in 2020." A inverts the trend; B picks the exception year; C contradicts the spike.

Q23 — Supporting Statistic (Medium)

Passage: A coach claims morning practices improved fitness. Sprint times dropped from 13.2 to 12.4 seconds on average after the switch.

Question: Which choice best supports the coach's claim?

  • A. Evening practices were also scheduled.
  • B. The team bought new uniforms.
  • C. Sprint times worsened after the switch.
  • D. Average sprint times improved from 13.2 to 12.4 seconds.
Answer: D. Faster times directly evidence fitness gains. A is neutral scheduling; B is irrelevant; C contradicts the data.

Q24 — Inference: Motive (Hard)

Passage: The company released the safety report on a Friday evening before a holiday weekend, when newsroom staffing is thinnest.

Question: Which inference is best supported?

  • A. The company wanted maximum publicity.
  • B. Safety reports are always released on Fridays.
  • C. Newsrooms are busiest on holiday weekends.
  • D. The timing suggests an attempt to minimize coverage.
Answer: D. Thin staffing + holiday eve implies burial. A inverts the logic; B generalizes once; C contradicts "thinnest."

Q25 — Combined Index (Hard)

Passage: Park scores (greenery + facilities): North: 8 + 6 = 14; South: 9 + 9 = 18; East: 10 + 5 = 15; West: 7 + 8 = 15.

Question: Which park scored highest overall?

  • A. North
  • B. South
  • C. East
  • D. West
Answer: B. 18 exceeds 14, 15, 15. A picks the lowest; C/D pick single-category highs (10 greenery, 8 facilities) over the total.

Q26 — Central Idea (Easy)

Passage: Volunteers removed 3 tons of plastic from the riverbank in a single weekend, the largest cleanup in the town's history.

Question: Which choice best states the main idea?

  • A. A record volunteer cleanup removed 3 tons of river plastic.
  • B. Plastic cannot be removed from rivers.
  • C. Weekends are too short for volunteering.
  • D. The town banned all volunteers.
Answer: A. Captures record + amount + event. B contradicts the success; C and D invent obstacles.

Q27 — Evidence Quality (Medium)

Passage: A blogger claims a supplement cures insomnia, citing three anonymous testimonials.

Question: Which choice best evaluates the evidence?

  • A. It is weak: anonymous anecdotes cannot establish a cure.
  • B. It is conclusive proof of a cure.
  • C. It proves insomnia is imaginary.
  • D. It shows supplements never work.
Answer: A. Three anonymous stories ≠ clinical proof. B overstates; C and D make absolute claims the evidence cannot touch.

Q28 — Data: Majority (Easy)

Passage: Survey of 1,000 residents: 620 support the new park, 250 oppose, 130 undecided.

Question: Which choice is true?

  • A. A majority supports the park.
  • B. Most residents oppose the park.
  • C. Nobody is undecided.
  • D. Support and opposition are tied.
Answer: A. 620/1000 = 62% majority. B inverts; C denies 130 undecided; D ignores the gap.

Q29 — Weaken (Hard)

Passage: A diet book claims its plan caused readers' weight loss, but adopters also began exercising daily at the same time.

Question: Which choice most weakens the book's causal claim?

  • A. Exercise began simultaneously, offering an alternative explanation.
  • B. Readers bought the book in large numbers.
  • C. The plan includes meal suggestions.
  • D. Weight loss is difficult to maintain.
Answer: A. A confound breaks the causal link. B reports sales, not causes; C describes content; D is a general truism.

Q30 — Stated Sequence (Medium)

Passage: Engineers first drained the tunnel, then inspected the lining, and finally restored water flow.

Question: What happened second?

  • A. Inspecting the lining.
  • B. Draining the tunnel.
  • C. Restoring water flow.
  • D. Building a new tunnel.
Answer: A. The order is drain → inspect → restore. B was first; C was third; D never happened.

Q31 — Proportional Reasoning (Medium)

Passage: A recipe uses 2 eggs for every 3 cups of flour. A baker uses 12 cups of flour.

Question: How many eggs are needed?

  • A. 6
  • B. 8
  • C. 9
  • D. 18
Answer: B. 12/3 = 4 batches × 2 = 8. A halves; C adds instead of scaling; D multiplies by 3 wrongly.

Q32 — Inference (Medium)

Passage: The island's bird counts fell each year after cats were introduced, then recovered after cats were removed.

Question: Which inference is best supported?

  • A. The cats likely contributed to the bird decline.
  • B. Birds caused the cats to arrive.
  • C. Counts never changed.
  • D. Removal had no effect.
Answer: A. Fall-then-recovery tracks the cats. B reverses causation; C denies the counts; D denies the recovery.

Q33 — Claim Evaluation (Hard)

Passage: A headline declares "Chocolate cures heart disease!" based on a study of 30 people with no control group.

Question: Which choice best evaluates the headline?

  • A. It overstates weak evidence from a tiny uncontrolled study.
  • B. It accurately reports conclusive proof.
  • C. It understates the findings.
  • D. It fairly describes a large trial.
Answer: A. n=30 with no controls cannot support "cures." B accepts hype; C inverts; D invents a large trial.

Q34 — Data: Change (Easy)

Passage: Bus fares rose from $2.00 to $2.50.

Question: What was the increase?

  • A. $0.50
  • B. $0.25
  • C. $4.50
  • D. 250%
Answer: A. 2.50 − 2.00 = 0.50. B halves; C adds; D misreads the new price as the percent.

Q35 — Best Summary (Medium)

Passage: Night-shift workers show higher rates of sleep disorders; controlled light exposure during shifts reduced symptoms in trials, suggesting schedules — not willpower — drive the risk.

Question: Which choice best summarizes the passage?

  • A. Light exposure trials suggest shift schedules drive sleep risk, and interventions help.
  • B. Willpower alone cures sleep disorders.
  • C. Night shifts have no health effects.
  • D. Trials proved light exposure is useless.
Answer: A. Captures finding + intervention. B contradicts "not willpower"; C denies the rates; D inverts the trial result.
